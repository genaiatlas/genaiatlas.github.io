    Business Context & Problem Statement

    Tourism Industry Challenge

    The tourism industry faces a fundamental challenge in customer acquisition and retention: identifying potential customers who are most likely to purchase travel packages. Traditional marketing approaches often involve random customer contact without leveraging available data insights, resulting in inefficient resource allocation and missed revenue opportunities.

    "Visit with us" Company Profile

    "Visit with us" operates as a tourism company offering five distinct package tiers: Basic, Standard, Deluxe, Super Deluxe, and King. Historical data reveals that only 18% of contacted customers ultimately purchase packages, indicating significant room for improvement in customer targeting strategies.

    Strategic Business Initiative

    The company plans to launch a Wellness Tourism Package - a specialized offering focused on travel experiences that maintain, enhance, or kick-start healthy lifestyles while supporting travelers' sense of well-being. This represents a strategic expansion into the growing wellness travel market, which requires precise customer targeting to ensure successful market penetration.

    Technical Problem Definition

    Primary Objective: Develop a predictive classification model to identify customers with the highest probability of purchasing the new wellness tourism package before initial contact.

    Business Impact: Enhanced customer targeting enables:

    • Resource Optimization: Focus marketing efforts on high-probability prospects

    • Revenue Maximization: Improve conversion rates from 18% baseline

    • Customer Experience: Provide relevant offerings to interested customers

    • Competitive Advantage: Data-driven approach versus industry's random targeting

    Data-Driven Approach: Leverage comprehensive customer profiles including demographics, interaction history, and behavioral patterns to predict purchase probability with measurable accuracy improvements.

    Success Metrics Framework

    Primary Metric: Recall (Sensitivity) - Maximizing identification of actual buyers

    • Business Rationale: Missing a potential customer (False Negative) represents lost revenue opportunity

    • Cost Analysis: Contacting a non-buyer (False Positive) costs marketing resources but missing a buyer costs entire package revenue

    Secondary Metrics: Precision, F1-Score, and overall accuracy for balanced performance assessment

    Data Overview & Quality Assessment

    Dataset Architecture

    The tourism dataset encompasses 4,888 customer records across 20 variables, providing a comprehensive view of customer demographics, preferences, and interaction history. This dataset represents one year of customer interactions and purchase decisions for existing package offerings.

    Variable Categories

    Customer Demographics (9 variables):

    • Age: Continuous variable representing customer age

    • Gender: Categorical (Male, Female) with data quality issues requiring correction

    • CityTier: Ordered categorical (Tier 1 > Tier 2 > Tier 3) representing city development level

    • Occupation: Categorical including Salaried, Small Business, Free Lancer

    • MaritalStatus: Categorical (Single, Married, Unmarried, Divorced)

    • MonthlyIncome: Continuous variable in local currency

    • Designation: Categorical representing professional hierarchy

    • Passport: Binary indicator (0: No, 1: Yes)

    • OwnCar: Binary indicator (0: No, 1: Yes)

    Travel Preferences (4 variables):

    • NumberOfPersonVisiting: Count of accompanying persons

    • PreferredPropertyStar: Hotel rating preference (1-5 stars)

    • NumberOfTrips: Annual trip frequency

    • NumberOfChildrenVisiting: Count of children under 5 years

    Sales Interaction Data (6 variables):

    • TypeofContact: Contact initiation method (Company Invited vs Self Inquiry)

    • ProductPitched: Package type offered during sales interaction

    • DurationOfPitch: Sales presentation duration in minutes

    • PitchSatisfactionScore: Customer satisfaction with sales presentation

    • NumberOfFollowups: Post-pitch follow-up count

    • Target Variable: ProdTaken (0: No Purchase, 1: Purchase)

    Data Quality Assessment

    Missing Value Pattern:

    • DurationOfPitch: 5.14% missing - indicates incomplete sales interaction records

    • MonthlyIncome: 4.77% missing - sensitive financial information collection challenges

    • Age: 4.62% missing - demographic data collection gaps

    • NumberOfTrips: 2.86% missing - historical travel behavior unavailable

    • NumberOfChildrenVisiting: 1.35% missing - family composition uncertainty

    • NumberOfFollowups: 0.92% missing - sales process tracking incomplete

    • PreferredPropertyStar: 0.53% missing - preference expression optional

    • TypeofContact: 0.51% missing - contact method documentation gaps

    Class Imbalance Challenge:

    The target variable shows severe imbalance with approximately 82% non-purchasers and 18% purchasers, creating modeling challenges that require specialized handling strategies.

    Data Quality Issues Identified

    Gender Data Inconsistency: 'Fe Male' entries requiring standardization to 'Female'

    Extreme Value Detection: Potential outliers in continuous variables requiring investigation

    Low-Frequency Categories: Some categorical variables contain extremely rare categories (e.g., Free Lancer occupation with only 2 instances)

    Temporal Limitations: Dataset lacks time-series information about customer journey progression

    Business Context Insights

    Customer Base Characteristics:

    • Geographic Distribution: Tier 1 cities dominate (65%), suggesting urban customer focus

    • Contact Initiation: 70% self-inquiry indicates strong brand awareness and interest

    • Economic Profile: Income distribution suggests middle-to-upper-middle-class targeting

    • Travel Behavior: Most customers plan group travel (2-4 persons), indicating family/social travel patterns

    Exploratory Data Analysis

    Univariate Analysis Insights

    Age Distribution Characteristics: Customer age follows an approximately normal distribution centered around 37 years, with minimal outliers. This distribution suggests the company successfully targets the prime travel demographic (25-50 years) who have both disposable income and travel motivation.

    Income Distribution Patterns: Monthly income exhibits a right-skewed distribution with most customers earning between 20,000-40,000 currency units. The presence of extreme values (both high and low) indicates diverse economic segments within the customer base, though the majority represents middle-class consumers.

    Travel Behavior Analysis: Number of trips per year shows a right-skewed distribution with 52% of customers taking 2-3 trips annually. This pattern indicates moderate travel frequency among the customer base, suggesting room for package upselling to increase trip frequency.

    Sales Interaction Duration: Pitch duration demonstrates right-skewed distribution with most presentations lasting under 20 minutes. Extreme outliers (duration > 40 minutes) represent only 2 observations, likely indicating special cases or data entry errors.

    Bivariate Relationship Analysis

    Marital Status Impact: Conversion analysis reveals higher purchase rates among single (unmarried) customers compared to married customers, despite married customers representing the majority of the customer base. This suggests package design or marketing messaging may be more appealing to single travelers.

    Product Pitch Effectiveness: Basic package pitches achieve the highest conversion rates, while Deluxe packages (most frequently pitched) show lower conversion rates than Standard packages. This finding suggests potential misalignment between product pitch frequency and customer preferences.

    Geographic Targeting Insights: Tier 1 city customers dominate the database but show varying conversion patterns. The unexpectedly high representation of Tier 3 cities compared to Tier 2 cities suggests either market penetration gaps or strategic focus differences.

    Passport Ownership Correlation: Customers with passports demonstrate significantly higher conversion rates, indicating international travel readiness strongly correlates with package purchase likelihood. This insight directly supports the wellness tourism package positioning.

    Professional Hierarchy Patterns: Executive-level customers show higher conversion rates compared to higher management levels (AVP/VP), suggesting different value propositions may be required for senior executives who may prefer premium, time-efficient travel experiences.

    Correlation Matrix Analysis

    Age-Income Relationship: Moderate positive correlation between age and monthly income aligns with career progression expectations, providing validation for demographic data quality.

    Age-Purchase Inverse Relationship: Weak negative correlation between age and purchase likelihood suggests younger customers are more receptive to package offerings, possibly due to higher discretionary spending or lifestyle preferences.

    Feature Independence: Limited high correlations between predictor variables indicate good feature diversity for modeling purposes, reducing multicollinearity concerns.

    Critical Limitations Identified

    Temporal Dynamics Missing: Static dataset lacks information about customer journey timing, seasonal variations, or purchase decision evolution, limiting understanding of customer behavior patterns.

    Interaction Context Gaps: Missing context about sales interaction quality, customer initial interest level, or competitive alternatives considered during decision-making process.

    Post-Purchase Behavior: No information about customer satisfaction, repeat purchase likelihood, or referral behavior to inform long-term value assessment.

    External Factor Exclusion: Absence of economic conditions, competitive landscape, or seasonal factors that may influence travel purchase decisions.

    Business Intelligence Insights

    Target Demographic Refinement: Analysis suggests optimal targeting focuses on single customers aged 25-40 with passport ownership and executive-level positions, representing the highest conversion probability segment.

    Product-Market Alignment: Gap between most-pitched products (Deluxe) and highest-converting products (Basic) indicates need for sales strategy refinement or product development focus.

    Geographic Expansion Opportunity: Under-representation of Tier 2 cities despite proximity to high-converting Tier 1 markets suggests untapped expansion potential.

    Data Preprocessing & Feature Engineering

    Outlier Detection and Treatment Strategy

    Systematic Outlier Identification: Statistical analysis identified extreme values in three continuous variables requiring intervention:

    • DurationOfPitch: 2 observations exceeding 40 minutes (likely data entry errors)

    • MonthlyIncome: 4 observations outside 12,000-40,000 range (extreme economic outliers)

    • NumberOfTrips: 4 observations exceeding 10 annual trips (likely business travelers or data errors)

    Treatment Rationale: Rather than imputation or transformation, complete removal was chosen because:

    • Low Impact: Affected observations represent <0.2% of total dataset

    • Data Quality: Extreme values likely represent measurement errors rather than legitimate business insights

    • Model Stability: Outlier removal prevents model distortion by extreme cases

    Business Impact Assessment: Outlier removal improves model generalization to typical customer populations while maintaining data integrity for mainstream customer segments.

    Missing Value Imputation Strategy

    Categorical Variable Imputation: Mode imputation applied to categorical variables based on business logic:

    • TypeofContact: Most frequent contact method reflects standard operational procedure

    • PreferredPropertyStar: Modal preference represents typical customer expectations

    • NumberOfChildrenVisiting: Mode imputation accounts for family travel patterns

    Continuous Variable Imputation: Median imputation selected for continuous variables due to:

    • Robustness: Median remains stable despite skewed distributions

    • Business Logic: Median represents typical customer characteristics better than mean

    • Distribution Preservation: Maintains underlying data distribution patterns

    Data Leakage Prevention: Imputation parameters calculated exclusively from training data and applied to validation/test sets, ensuring no information leakage from future data into model training.

    Feature Engineering Decisions

    Variable Exclusion Strategy: Four variables removed from modeling due to temporal availability constraints:

    • DurationOfPitch: Unavailable before customer contact

    • NumberOfFollowups: Represents post-contact interaction

    • ProductPitched: Sales strategy decision, not customer characteristic

    • PitchSatisfactionScore: Post-interaction feedback unavailable during prediction

    Exclusion Rationale: The model must predict purchase likelihood before initial customer contact, requiring exclusion of any post-contact interaction variables.

    Categorical Encoding Implementation: One-hot encoding applied to categorical variables with drop_first=True to prevent multicollinearity while preserving categorical information for model interpretation.

    Data Splitting Strategy

    Stratified Sampling Approach: 70-30 train-test split with stratification ensures:

    • Class Balance Preservation: Maintains 18% purchase rate in both sets

    • Representative Sampling: Both sets reflect overall population characteristics

    • Robust Evaluation: Test set provides unbiased performance estimation

    Validation Strategy Considerations: Single train-test split chosen over cross-validation due to sufficient sample size (4,888 observations) and need for consistent final model evaluation.

    Feature Scaling Considerations

    SVM-Specific Scaling: MinMax scaling (-1 to 1 range) applied specifically for Support Vector Machine algorithms due to:

    • Distance-Based Sensitivity: SVM performance depends on feature scale uniformity

    • Optimization Efficiency: Scaled features improve convergence speed

    • Numerical Stability: Prevents large-scale features from dominating decision boundaries

    Tree-Based Model Advantage: Decision Tree and Random Forest models require no scaling due to their split-based decision mechanisms being scale-invariant.

    Preprocessing Pipeline Validation

    Quality Assurance Checks: Post-preprocessing validation confirms:

    • Zero Missing Values: All variables complete across train/test sets

    • Consistent Encoding: Categorical variables properly transformed

    • Data Type Alignment: Numerical variables properly formatted for modeling

    • Shape Consistency: Feature dimensionality matches across datasets

    Preprocessing Limitations

    Information Loss: Outlier removal eliminates potentially valuable edge cases that might represent high-value customer segments or unique market opportunities.

    Imputation Assumptions: Mode and median imputation assumes missing data patterns are random rather than systematic, which may not hold for sensitive variables like income.

    Feature Reduction Impact: Exclusion of post-contact variables limits model's ability to learn from complete customer interaction history, potentially reducing predictive power.

    Temporal Static Assumption: Preprocessing assumes customer characteristics remain stable over time, which may not reflect evolving customer preferences or life circumstances.

    Model Development & Comparison

    Algorithm Selection Strategy

    Four classification algorithms were selected to capture different aspects of customer purchase behavior:

    Logistic Regression: Linear baseline model for interpretable probability estimation

    Support Vector Machine: Non-linear pattern recognition with kernel flexibility

    Decision Tree: Rule-based interpretation with clear decision pathways

    Random Forest: Ensemble method for improved generalization and feature importance

    Evaluation Metrics Framework

    Primary Metric: Recall (Sensitivity) Business priority focuses on maximizing recall because:

    • False Negative Cost: Missing a potential buyer represents lost revenue (entire package value)

    • False Positive Cost: Contacting a non-buyer costs only marketing resources

    • Business Impact: Revenue opportunity cost significantly exceeds marketing cost

    Supporting Metrics: Precision, F1-Score, and accuracy provide balanced performance assessment while maintaining recall priority.

    Logistic Regression Performance Analysis

    Baseline Performance: - Training Accuracy: 82% with perfect specificity but poor recall (25%)

    • Test Performance: Similar pattern with 23% recall, indicating consistent underperformance in identifying buyers

    Threshold Optimization Results: Precision-Recall curve analysis identified optimal threshold of 0.25, improving:

    • Recall Enhancement: From 23% to 67% (+44% improvement)

    • Precision Trade-off: Decreased from higher baseline but maintained business-acceptable levels

    • Business Impact: Significantly improved buyer identification capability

    Model Interpretation: Linear decision boundary may be insufficient for complex customer behavior patterns, suggesting need for non-linear approaches.

    Support Vector Machine Analysis

    Linear Kernel Limitations: Initial SVM with linear kernel and default threshold showed catastrophic failure:

    • Complete Class Blindness: Predicted all customers as non-buyers (0% recall)

    • Imbalanced Data Impact: Class imbalance caused model to default to majority class

    • Threshold Sensitivity: Default 0.5 threshold inappropriate for imbalanced classification

    Threshold Optimization Impact: Optimal threshold of 0.25 dramatically improved performance:

    • Recall Recovery: From 0% to 61% with threshold adjustment

    • Generalization: Similar performance on train/test sets indicating good generalization

    • F1-Score Improvement: From 0.00 to 0.52 demonstrating balanced performance recovery

    RBF Kernel Enhancement: Non-linear RBF kernel with optimized threshold (0.17) achieved:

    • Superior Recall: 69% recall representing best performance among tested models

    • Balanced Metrics: Maintained reasonable precision while maximizing recall

    • Business Applicability: Optimal performance for business objective of maximizing buyer identification

    Decision Tree Performance Evaluation

    Default Model Overfitting: Unpruned decision tree exhibited classic overfitting symptoms:

    • Perfect Training Performance: Near 100% accuracy on training data

    • Generalization Failure: Significant performance degradation on test set

    • Complexity Overreach: Model memorized training patterns rather than learning generalizable rules

    Hyperparameter Tuning Results: GridSearchCV optimization with 5-fold cross-validation found optimal parameters:

    • Complexity Control: max_depth, max_leaf_nodes, min_samples_split tuning

    • Overfitting Reduction: Improved generalization through pruning

    • Performance Balance: Better test performance with slight training performance reduction

    Feature Importance Insights: Tuned decision tree identified key predictive features:

    • MonthlyIncome: Primary decision factor

    • Age: Secondary demographic influence

    • NumberOfTrips: Behavioral pattern significance

    Random Forest Ensemble Performance

    Initial Performance Assessment: Random Forest showed typical ensemble behavior:

    • Training Excellence: Near-perfect training performance indicating high model capacity

    • Overfitting Tendency: Performance gap between train/test sets

    • Moderate Recall: 47% recall below SVM performance

    Feature Importance Validation: Random Forest confirmed decision tree insights regarding important features:

    • Income Dominance: Monthly income as primary predictive factor

    • Age Significance: Demographic importance validation

    • Trip Behavior: Travel history predictive value

    Ensemble Limitations: Despite reputation for overfitting resistance, Random Forest showed:

    • Insufficient Recall: Below business requirements for buyer identification

    • Tuning Necessity: Default parameters inadequate for imbalanced classification

    Model Comparison Synthesis

    Performance Ranking by Recall: 1. SVM with RBF Kernel: 69% recall (optimal for business objective) 2. Logistic Regression: 67% recall (close second with better interpretability) 3. SVM with Linear Kernel: 61% recall (threshold-dependent improvement) 4. Random Forest: 47% recall (below business requirements) 5. Decision Tree: Variable recall with overfitting concerns

    Business Model Selection: SVM with RBF kernel emerges as optimal choice based on:

    • Highest Recall: Best buyer identification capability
    • Balanced Performance: Reasonable precision maintenance
    • Generalization: Consistent train/test performance
    • Business Alignment: Maximizes revenue opportunity identification

    Critical Model Limitations

    Imbalanced Data Challenge: All models struggled with severe class imbalance (18% purchase rate), requiring threshold optimization rather than architectural solutions.

    Feature Engineering Gaps: Limited feature engineering may have constrained model performance, particularly interaction terms or derived features.

    Cross-Validation Absence: Single train-test split may not provide robust performance estimates compared to k-fold cross-validation approaches.

    Ensemble Exploration: Limited exploration of advanced ensemble methods (XGBoost, stacking) that might achieve superior performance.

    Advanced Model Optimization

    Threshold Optimization Methodology

    Precision-Recall Curve Analysis: Systematic threshold optimization employed precision-recall curves to identify optimal operating points for each model, prioritizing recall maximization while maintaining business-acceptable precision levels.

    Business-Driven Threshold Selection: Threshold selection considered business cost structure:

    • Revenue Opportunity Cost: Missing a buyer costs entire package revenue

    • Marketing Cost: Contacting non-buyer costs limited marketing resources

    • ROI Optimization: Threshold balances recall gains against precision losses

    Logistic Regression Optimization

    Threshold Sensitivity Analysis: Comprehensive threshold testing from 0.1 to 0.9 revealed:

    • Optimal Point: 0.25 threshold providing 67% recall with acceptable precision

    • Trade-off Curve: Clear inverse relationship between precision and recall

    • Business Sweet Spot: 0.25 represents optimal business ROI balance

    Performance Improvement Quantification: - Recall Enhancement: 44 percentage point improvement (23% → 67%)

    • Revenue Impact: Potential to identify 67% of buyers versus 23% with default threshold

    • Customer Reach: Expanded targetable customer segment significantly

    Support Vector Machine Optimization Journey

    Linear Kernel Recovery: Initial catastrophic failure (0% recall) completely recovered through threshold optimization:

    • Threshold Impact: 0.25 threshold enabled 61% recall achievement

    • Class Balance Resolution: Addressed severe imbalanced data effects

    • Performance Validation: Consistent train/test performance confirmed generalization

    RBF Kernel Enhancement: Advanced to non-linear RBF kernel with aggressive threshold optimization:

    • Optimal Threshold: 0.17 (lower than linear kernel) for maximum recall

    • Performance Peak: 69% recall representing best overall model performance

    • Non-Linear Advantage: RBF kernel captured complex customer behavior patterns

    • Business Champion: Selected as final model for deployment

    Decision Tree Hyperparameter Tuning

    GridSearchCV Implementation: Comprehensive parameter space exploration included:

    • max_depth: Range 1-100 with 10-unit steps for complexity control

    • max_leaf_nodes: [50, 75, 150, 250] for terminal node limitation

    • min_samples_split: [10, 30, 50, 70] for split threshold control

    • Cross-Validation: 5-fold CV for robust parameter evaluation

    Overfitting Mitigation: Tuning successfully reduced overfitting through:

    • Complexity Reduction: Optimal parameters prevented training data memorization

    • Generalization Improvement: Better test set performance with controlled training performance

    • Interpretability Maintenance: Pruned tree remained business-interpretable

    Optimal Configuration Results: Best parameters achieved balanced performance while maintaining decision tree interpretability advantages for business stakeholder communication.

    Feature Importance Analysis

    Consistent Ranking Validation: Both Decision Tree and Random Forest confirmed identical feature importance ranking:

    1. MonthlyIncome: Dominant predictive factor indicating economic capacity primacy

    2. Age: Secondary demographic influence reflecting life stage travel patterns

    3. NumberOfTrips: Behavioral indicator of travel propensity and experience

    Business Intelligence Validation: Feature importance results align with tourism industry knowledge:

    • Economic Capacity: Income directly relates to travel affordability

    • Life Stage: Age correlates with discretionary spending and travel motivation

    • Travel Experience: Previous trip frequency indicates travel comfort and interest

    Model Selection Rationale

    SVM RBF Kernel Selection: Final model selection based on comprehensive evaluation:

    • Primary Objective: Highest recall (69%) maximizing buyer identification

    • Generalization: Consistent performance across train/test sets

    • Business Alignment: Optimal revenue opportunity maximization

    • Robustness: Effective handling of imbalanced classification challenge

    Alternative Model Considerations: - Logistic Regression: Close second with 67% recall and superior interpretability

    • Decision Tree: High interpretability but lower recall performance

    • Random Forest: Insufficient recall for business requirements

    Optimization Limitations and Risks

    Precision-Recall Curve Analysis Framework

    def analyze_precision_recall_curve(model, X_train, y_train, model_name):
    """
    Comprehensive precision-recall analysis for threshold optimization

    Parameters:
    -----------
    model : sklearn estimator
        Trained classification model with predict_proba method
    X_train : DataFrame/array
        Training features
    y_train : Series/array
        Training target variable
    model_name : str
        Model identifier for plotting

    Returns:
    --------
    dict : Optimal threshold and performance metrics
    """
    # Generate probability predictions
    y_scores = model.predict_proba(X_train)[:, 1]

    # Calculate precision-recall curve
    precisions, recalls, thresholds = precision_recall_curve(y_train, y_scores)

    # Plot precision-recall curve
    plt.figure(figsize=(10, 7))
    plt.plot(thresholds, precisions[:-1], 'b--', label='Precision', linewidth=2)
    plt.plot(thresholds, recalls[:-1], 'g--', label='Recall', linewidth=2)
    plt.xlabel('Threshold', fontsize=12)
    plt.ylabel('Score', fontsize=12)
    plt.title(f'Precision-Recall Curve: {model_name}', fontsize=14)
    plt.legend(loc='upper right', fontsize=11)
    plt.grid(True, alpha=0.3)
    plt.ylim([0, 1])
    plt.show()

    # Find optimal threshold for business objective (maximize recall with acceptable precision)
    f1_scores = 2 * (precisions[:-1] * recalls[:-1]) / (precisions[:-1] + recalls[:-1])

    # Business logic: prioritize recall but maintain minimum precision of 0.3
    viable_thresholds = thresholds[precisions[:-1] >= 0.3]
    viable_recalls = recalls[:-1][precisions[:-1] >= 0.3]

    if len(viable_thresholds) > 0:
        optimal_idx = np.argmax(viable_recalls)
        optimal_threshold = viable_thresholds[optimal_idx]
    else:
        # If no threshold meets precision requirement, choose best F1 score
        optimal_idx = np.argmax(f1_scores)
        optimal_threshold = thresholds[optimal_idx]

    return {
        'optimal_threshold': optimal_threshold,
        'precision_at_optimal': precisions[:-1][optimal_idx],
        'recall_at_optimal': recalls[:-1][optimal_idx],
        'f1_at_optimal': f1_scores[optimal_idx]
    }

    Purpose: Provides systematic framework for threshold optimization balancing business requirements (recall) with acceptable precision levels.

    Flow: - Probability Generation: Extract class probabilities for threshold analysis - Curve Calculation: Generate precision-recall trade-off curves - Visualization: Plot trade-off relationships for stakeholder communication - Business Logic: Apply business constraints (minimum precision) to threshold selection - Optimization: Select threshold maximizing recall within business constraints

    Impact: Transforms statistical model outputs into business-optimized decision systems. Framework enables consistent optimization across all models.

    class TravelPackagePredictionSystem:
    """
    Production-ready travel package purchase prediction system

    Attributes:
    -----------
    model : sklearn estimator
        Trained classification model
    threshold : float
        Optimized decision threshold
    feature_names : list
        Expected feature names for input validation
    preprocessing_pipeline : dict
        Preprocessing steps and parameters
    performance_baseline : dict
        Baseline performance metrics for monitoring
    """

    def __init__(self, model, threshold, feature_names, preprocessing_pipeline, baseline_metrics):
        self.model = model
        self.threshold = threshold
        self.feature_names = feature_names
        self.preprocessing_pipeline = preprocessing_pipeline
        self.baseline_metrics = baseline_metrics
        self.prediction_log = []
        self.performance_history = []

    def validate_input_data(self, customer_data):
        """
        Validates input customer data for prediction

        Parameters:
        -----------
        customer_data : DataFrame
            Customer information for prediction

        Returns:
        --------
        bool : True if valid, raises exception if invalid
        """
        # Check required columns
        missing_cols = set(self.feature_names) - set(customer_data.columns)
        if missing_cols:
            raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {missing_cols}")

        # Check data types
        numeric_cols = ['Age', 'MonthlyIncome', 'NumberOfTrips', 'NumberOfPersonVisiting', 
                       'PreferredPropertyStar', 'NumberOfChildrenVisiting', 'Passport', 'OwnCar']

        for col in numeric_cols:
            if col in customer_data.columns:
                if not pd.api.types.is_numeric_dtype(customer_data[col]):
                    raise ValueError(f"Column {col} must be numeric")

        # Business logic validation
        if 'Age' in customer_data.columns:
            invalid_ages = (customer_data['Age'] < 18) | (customer_data['Age'] > 100)
            if invalid_ages.any():
                raise ValueError("Age must be between 18 and 100")

        if 'MonthlyIncome' in customer_data.columns:
            invalid_income = customer_data['MonthlyIncome'] < 0
            if invalid_income.any():
                raise ValueError("Monthly income cannot be negative")

        return True

    def preprocess_customer_data(self, customer_data):
        """
        Applies preprocessing steps to customer data

        Parameters:
        -----------
        customer_data : DataFrame
            Raw customer data

        Returns:
        --------
        DataFrame : Preprocessed data ready for prediction
        """
        processed_data = customer_data.copy()

        # Handle missing values using training-derived parameters
        if 'median_imputer' in self.preprocessing_pipeline:
            median_cols = self.preprocessing_pipeline['median_imputer']['columns']
            median_values = self.preprocessing_pipeline['median_imputer']['values']

            for col, median_val in zip(median_cols, median_values):
                if col in processed_data.columns:
                    processed_data[col].fillna(median_val, inplace=True)

        if 'mode_imputer' in self.preprocessing_pipeline:
            mode_cols = self.preprocessing_pipeline['mode_imputer']['columns']
            mode_values = self.preprocessing_pipeline['mode_imputer']['values']

            for col, mode_val in zip(mode_cols, mode_values):
                if col in processed_data.columns:
                    processed_data[col].fillna(mode_val, inplace=True)

        # Apply categorical encoding
        if 'categorical_encoding' in self.preprocessing_pipeline:
            encoded_features = self.preprocessing_pipeline['categorical_encoding']['feature_names']

            # Create dummy variables for categorical columns
            categorical_cols = ['TypeofContact', 'Occupation', 'Gender', 'MaritalStatus', 'Designation', 'CityTier']

            for col in categorical_cols:
                if col in processed_data.columns:
                    dummies = pd.get_dummies(processed_data[col], prefix=col, drop_first=True)
                    processed_data = pd.concat([processed_data, dummies], axis=1)
                    processed_data.drop(columns=[col], inplace=True)

        # Ensure all required features are present
        for feature in self.feature_names:
            if feature not in processed_data.columns:
                processed_data[feature] = 0  # Add missing encoded features as 0

        # Reorder columns to match training data
        processed_data = processed_data[self.feature_names]

        return processed_data

    def predict_purchase_probability(self, customer_data):
        """
        Predicts purchase probability for new customers

        Parameters:
        -----------
        customer_data : DataFrame
            Customer information

        Returns:
        --------
        dict : Prediction results with business insights
        """
        # Validate and preprocess input
        self.validate_input_data(customer_data)
        processed_data = self.preprocess_customer_data(customer_data)

        # Generate predictions
        probabilities = self.model.predict_proba(processed_data)[:, 1]
        predictions = (probabilities > self.threshold).astype(int)

        # Calculate confidence scores
        confidence_scores = np.where(
            predictions == 1,
            probabilities,  # For positive predictions, confidence = probability
            1 - probabilities  # For negative predictions, confidence = 1 - probability
        )

        # Create results
        results = []
        for i in range(len(customer_data)):
            customer_result = {
                'customer_id': i,
                'purchase_probability': round(probabilities[i], 3),
                'predicted_purchase': bool(predictions[i]),
                'confidence_score': round(confidence_scores[i], 3),
                'risk_level': self.categorize_risk_level(probabilities[i]),
                'recommended_action': self.get_recommendation(probabilities[i], predictions[i]),
                'segment': self.identify_customer_segment(customer_data.iloc[i])
            }
            results.append(customer_result)

        # Log predictions for monitoring
        self.log_predictions(customer_data, results)

        return results

    def categorize_risk_level(self, probability):
        """Categorizes customer risk level based on purchase probability"""
        if probability >= 0.7:
            return "High Conversion Risk"
        elif probability >= 0.4:
            return "Medium Conversion Risk"
        elif probability >= 0.2:
            return "Low Conversion Risk"
        else:
            return "Very Low Conversion Risk"

    def get_recommendation(self, probability, prediction):
        """Provides business recommendation based on prediction"""
        if prediction == 1 and probability >= 0.7:
            return "High Priority: Contact with premium package offerings"
        elif prediction == 1 and probability >= 0.4:
            return "Medium Priority: Contact with standard package offerings"
        elif probability >= 0.2:
            return "Low Priority: Include in broad marketing campaigns"
        else:
            return "Very Low Priority: Focus resources on higher probability customers"

    def identify_customer_segment(self, customer_row):
        """Identifies customer segment based on characteristics"""
        income = customer_row.get('MonthlyIncome', 0)
        age = customer_row.get('Age', 0)
        trips = customer_row.get('NumberOfTrips', 0)

        if income >= 30000 and 30 <= age <= 50 and trips >= 3:
            return "High-Value Travelers"
        elif income >= 20000 and age < 35 and trips >= 1:
            return "Emerging Travelers"
        else:
            return "Budget-Conscious"

    def log_predictions(self, input_data, results):
        """Logs predictions for performance monitoring"""
        log_entry = {
            'timestamp': pd.Timestamp.now(),
            'customer_count': len(input_data),
            'high_probability_count': sum(1 for r in results if r['purchase_probability'] >= 0.5),
            'predictions_made': len(results)
        }
        self.prediction_log.append(log_entry)
