    Problem Statement & Business Context

    Business Challenge

    Thera bank faces a critical challenge with declining credit card usage, directly impacting revenue streams from various fee structures including annual fees, balance transfer costs, cash advance fees, and transaction-based charges. Customer attrition in credit card services represents significant financial losses, as acquiring new customers costs substantially more than retaining existing ones.

    Strategic Objective

    The primary goal is developing a predictive classification model to identify customers at high risk of churning, enabling proactive retention strategies. This analysis focuses on understanding both the predictive patterns and underlying drivers of customer attrition.

    Business Impact Framework

    Revenue Protection: Credit cards generate revenue through multiple channels, making customer retention crucial for maintaining profitability.

    Cost Optimization: Early identification of at-risk customers enables targeted retention efforts, optimizing marketing spend and customer service resources.

    Competitive Advantage: Understanding churn drivers provides insights for service improvements and competitive positioning.

    Dataset Understanding & Feature Analysis

    Dataset Composition

    The analysis utilizes a comprehensive dataset of 10,127 credit card customers with 21 features, providing rich information about customer demographics, financial behavior, and engagement patterns.

    Critical Feature Categories

    Customer Demographics: Age, gender, education level, marital status, dependent count, and income category provide socioeconomic context influencing financial behavior.

    Financial Profile: Credit limit, revolving balance, and utilization ratios reveal customer financial capacity and credit usage patterns.

    Behavioral Metrics: Transaction amounts, transaction counts, relationship tenure, and product usage indicate engagement levels and bank relationship depth.

    Engagement Indicators: Contact frequency, months inactive, and relationship count measure customer-bank interaction intensity.

    Feature Interpretation Framework

    Revolving Balance Dynamics: Unpaid monthly balances that carry over indicate customer borrowing behavior and potential financial stress indicators.

    Open-to-Buy Analysis: Available credit remaining (Credit_Limit - Current_Balance) reflects customer spending capacity and risk tolerance.

    Utilization Ratio Significance: The proportion of used credit (Total_Revolving_Bal / Credit_Limit) is crucial for creditworthiness assessment and spending pattern analysis.

    Data Quality Assessment

    Initial data exploration reveals missing values in Education_Level (15%) and Marital_Status (7%), requiring systematic imputation strategies. The dataset also contains 'abc' values in Income_Category that represent disguised missing data requiring special handling.

    Exploratory Data Analysis & Insights

    Univariate Distribution Analysis

    Age Distribution: Customer ages follow a normal distribution centered at 46 years, indicating a mature customer base with stable financial profiles. The broad age range (26-73) suggests diverse life stages and corresponding financial needs.

    Tenure Patterns: Most customers maintain 3-year relationships with the bank, indicating established loyalty patterns. However, the presence of outliers on both extremes suggests varied relationship development trajectories.

    Credit Limit Segmentation: The right-skewed distribution reveals most customers have modest credit limits (<\(5,000), while a small segment enjoys premium limits (>\)30,000), creating distinct customer tiers.

    Transaction Behavior: Bimodal transaction count distribution shows two distinct usage patterns - moderate users (40 transactions/year) and active users (80 transactions/year), suggesting different engagement levels.

    Utilization Patterns: Right-skewed utilization ratios indicate most customers use minimal credit (<27%), presenting opportunities for increased engagement and revenue generation.

    Bivariate Relationship Discovery

    Gender-Based Patterns: Churn rates remain relatively consistent across gender (20% for both males and females), indicating gender-neutral risk factors.

    Education Impact: Higher education levels (Doctorate, Post-Graduate) show increased churn rates (~20%), suggesting sophisticated customers may have higher service expectations or alternative options.

    Income Extremes: Both low-income (<\(40K) and high-income (>\)120K) segments exhibit elevated churn, indicating different underlying causes - financial stress versus service expectations.

    Premium Card Risk: Platinum (35%) and Gold (30%) cardholders show alarming attrition rates, representing significant revenue risk as these are the bank's most profitable customers.

    Engagement Paradox: Customers with highest bank contact frequency show elevated churn, suggesting service resolution failures rather than proactive engagement success.

    Critical Business Insights

    Relationship Depth Protection: Customers using multiple bank products (>3) show dramatically lower churn rates, highlighting cross-selling importance for retention.

    Activity-Retention Correlation: Strong negative correlation between transaction activity (both count and amount) and churn probability validates engagement-based retention strategies.

    Inactivity Risk Escalation: 2-4 months of inactivity creates the highest churn risk, establishing clear early warning indicators.

    Data Preprocessing & Feature Engineering

    Missing Data Strategy

    Systematic Imputation Approach: Missing values in categorical variables (Education_Level, Marital_Status, Income_Category) are addressed using mode imputation, maintaining distributional properties while avoiding arbitrary assumptions.

    Data Quality Enhancement: The 'abc' values in Income_Category are properly identified as missing data and converted to null values before imputation, ensuring consistent data treatment.

    Feature Engineering Decisions

    Target Variable Encoding: Attrition_Flag is converted to binary format (0=Existing, 1=Attrited) for classification model compatibility.

    Categorical Variable Treatment: One-hot encoding transforms categorical variables into numerical representations while avoiding multicollinearity through drop_first=True parameter.

    Feature Scaling Considerations: Different algorithms require different scaling approaches - standardization for SVM models while tree-based models handle raw features effectively.

    Train-Test Split Strategy

    Stratified Sampling Implementation: 70-30 train-test split uses stratified sampling to maintain class distribution balance, crucial for imbalanced classification problems where churn represents minority class.

    Data Leakage Prevention: Preprocessing steps (imputation, encoding) are fitted on training data and applied to test data, preventing information leakage that could inflate model performance estimates.

    Feature Space Transformation

    After preprocessing, the feature space expands to 29 dimensions through one-hot encoding, creating a comprehensive representation of customer characteristics while maintaining model interpretability.

    The preprocessing pipeline ensures robust model training while preserving the underlying data relationships essential for accurate churn prediction.

    Model Building & Algorithm Comparison

    Algorithm Selection Rationale

    Four distinct algorithms are evaluated to capture different aspects of the churn prediction problem:

    Logistic Regression: Provides interpretable linear relationships and coefficient analysis for understanding feature importance and business driver identification.

    Support Vector Machines: Handles non-linear relationships through kernel functions, potentially capturing complex customer behavior patterns.

    Decision Trees: Offers intuitive rule-based decisions that translate directly to business logic and customer segmentation strategies.

    Random Forest: Combines multiple decision trees to improve prediction accuracy while maintaining feature importance insights.

    Model Performance Framework

    Evaluation Metric Priority: Recall is prioritized over accuracy because misclassifying a churning customer (False Negative) costs significantly more than misclassifying a loyal customer (False Positive). The business objective focuses on identifying all potential churners for retention efforts.

    Threshold Optimization: Default prediction thresholds are adjusted using precision-recall curves to maximize business value rather than statistical accuracy.

    Algorithm-Specific Insights

    Logistic Regression Results: Achieves 90% accuracy but only 44% recall for churn identification. Coefficient analysis reveals contact frequency, inactivity, and dependent count as primary churn drivers. Optimal threshold adjustment improves recall to acceptable levels.

    SVM Performance: Linear kernel provides similar performance to logistic regression. RBF kernel with optimized threshold (0.31) achieves 77% recall, representing a significant improvement for business applications.

    Decision Tree Analysis: Shows slight overfitting tendencies but provides clear feature importance rankings. Total_Trans_Ct emerges as the most critical feature, followed by Total_Revolving_Bal and Total_Trans_Amt.

    Random Forest Results: Demonstrates overfitting with perfect training performance but 80% test recall. Feature importance analysis confirms transaction-based features as primary churn predictors.

    Business-Technical Translation

    The models reveal that customer engagement metrics (transactions, relationship depth) are more predictive than demographic characteristics, suggesting behavioral interventions will be more effective than demographic targeting.

    Performance Evaluation & Optimization

    Evaluation Methodology

    Confusion Matrix Analysis: Each model's performance is evaluated using confusion matrices to understand the trade-offs between precision and recall, particularly focusing on false negative reduction.

    Precision-Recall Trade-off: Threshold optimization balances precision and recall to maximize business value. The analysis shows optimal thresholds typically fall between 0.31-0.35, significantly lower than the default 0.5.

    Cross-Validation Considerations: Stratified sampling ensures consistent class distribution across train-test splits, providing reliable performance estimates despite class imbalance.

    Performance Benchmarking

    Baseline Accuracy: All models achieve approximately 90% accuracy, but this metric is misleading given the class imbalance (majority non-churning customers).

    Recall Improvement: Threshold optimization improves recall from 40-55% to 70-77% across models, representing a 25-40% improvement in churn identification capability.

    Precision Trade-offs: Improved recall comes at the cost of precision, but the business impact analysis supports this trade-off given the higher cost of missed churners versus false alarms.

    Model Selection Framework

    SVM RBF Optimal Performance: The SVM with RBF kernel and optimized threshold provides the best balance of recall (77%) and generalization, making it the recommended model for deployment.

    Feature Importance Consensus: Both tree-based models and logistic regression identify similar key features, providing confidence in feature importance rankings.

    Deployment Readiness Assessment

    Generalization Capability: Models show consistent performance across train-test splits, indicating good generalization potential for new customer data.

    Computational Efficiency: All models demonstrate reasonable training and prediction times suitable for batch processing of customer databases.

    Model Interpretability: Feature importance rankings and coefficient analysis provide clear business insights for strategic decision-making.

    Critical Analysis & Limitations

    Model Performance Limitations

    Class Imbalance Impact: Despite stratified sampling, the inherent class imbalance (20% churn rate) limits model performance. Advanced techniques like SMOTE or cost-sensitive learning could improve results.

    Feature Engineering Gaps: The analysis relies primarily on engineered features from existing data. Additional external data sources (economic indicators, competitor actions) could enhance predictive power.

    Temporal Considerations: The dataset represents a snapshot without time-series analysis. Customer behavior evolution over time is not captured, potentially missing dynamic churn patterns.

    Methodology Constraints

    Correlation vs. Causation: While models identify predictive features, they don't establish causal relationships. Business interventions based on correlations may not achieve expected results.

    Threshold Sensitivity: Optimal thresholds are determined on historical data and may require periodic recalibration as customer behavior evolves.

    Overfitting Risks: Random Forest shows clear overfitting tendencies, and Decision Tree performance gaps suggest model complexity may exceed optimal levels for generalization.

    Business Application Challenges

    Implementation Costs: Acting on all model predictions may be costly. A cost-benefit analysis framework is needed to optimize intervention targeting.

    Customer Privacy: Intensive monitoring and intervention programs must balance effectiveness with customer privacy and satisfaction concerns.

    Market Dynamics: Banking industry changes, competitive actions, and economic conditions could rapidly alter the relevance of current patterns.

    Data Quality Considerations

    Missing Data Impact: 15% missing education data and disguised missing income categories may introduce bias despite imputation efforts.

    Feature Completeness: The dataset lacks some potentially important features like credit score, payment history, or competitor offerings that could improve predictions.

    Sample Representativeness: The dataset's representativeness of the broader customer base and temporal stability requires validation.

    Recommendations for Improvement

    Advanced Techniques: Implement ensemble methods, deep learning approaches, or time-series analysis for improved performance.

    External Data Integration: Incorporate economic indicators, competitor analysis, and market trend data.

    Continuous Learning: Establish model monitoring and retraining pipelines to maintain predictive accuracy over time.

    A/B Testing Framework: Implement controlled experiments to validate business interventions derived from model insights.

    # CLIENTNUM consists of unique ID for clients and hence will not add value to the modeling

def calculate_risk_scores(model, X_data, customer_ids=None):

    Performance Monitoring Setup

    # Model Performance Monitoring Framework
class ChurnModelMonitor:
    def __init__(self, model, threshold=0.31):
        self.model = model
        self.threshold = threshold
        self.performance_history = []

    def evaluate_batch(self, X_new, y_true):
        # Generate predictions
        y_pred_proba = self.model.predict_proba(X_new)[:, 1]
        y_pred = y_pred_proba > self.threshold

        # Calculate metrics
        accuracy = accuracy_score(y_true, y_pred)
        precision = precision_score(y_true, y_pred)
        recall = recall_score(y_true, y_pred)

        # Store results
        batch_results = {
            'timestamp': pd.Timestamp.now(),
            'accuracy': accuracy,
            'precision': precision,
            'recall': recall,
            'sample_size': len(y_true)
        }

        self.performance_history.append(batch_results)
        return batch_results

    def detect_drift(self, threshold_recall=0.70):
        if len(self.performance_history) < 2:
            return False

        recent_recall = self.performance_history[-1]['recall']
        return recent_recall < threshold_recall

# Initialize monitoring
monitor = ChurnModelMonitor(svm_rbf, threshold=0.31)

    Purpose: Production monitoring framework detects model performance degradation and triggers retraining workflows.

    Flow: Systematic performance tracking with drift detection enables proactive model maintenance.

    Impact: Monitoring prevents silent model failure and maintains prediction accuracy in production environments.
