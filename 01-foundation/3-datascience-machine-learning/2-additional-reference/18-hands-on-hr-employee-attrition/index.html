    Business Context & Objectives

    The Challenge

    McCurr Health Consultancy faces a critical HR challenge that many multinational corporations encounter: high employee attrition costs. The company currently invests heavily in retention initiatives across all employees, but the Head of People Operations seeks a more targeted, cost-effective approach.

    Strategic Objective

    Transform from blanket retention spending to predictive, targeted retention by identifying employees at highest risk of attrition before they leave.

    Expected Business Impact

    • Cost Reduction: Focus retention incentives only on at-risk employees

    • Talent Retention: Prevent loss of valuable employees through early intervention

    • Resource Optimization: Allocate HR resources more strategically

    • Competitive Advantage: Maintain workforce stability in competitive markets

    Success Metrics

    The project's success will be measured by the model's ability to correctly identify employees who will leave (high recall for attrition class), minimizing false negatives that result in losing valuable talent.

    Dataset Overview & Structure

    Data Characteristics

    The dataset contains 2,940 employee records with 34 variables covering three key areas:

    • Demographic Information: Age, gender, marital status, education
    • Work-Related Metrics: Job role, department, performance ratings, satisfaction scores
    • Compensation & Benefits: Income, salary hikes, stock options, overtime status

    Target Variable Analysis

    Attrition Rate: 16% of employees in the dataset have left the organization, indicating a moderate but concerning attrition rate that aligns with industry averages for knowledge-based organizations.

    Data Quality Assessment

    Strengths: - Complete Dataset: No missing values across all 2,940 records - Rich Feature Set: Comprehensive mix of categorical and numerical variables - Balanced Coverage: Good representation of different departments and job levels

    Limitations: - Class Imbalance: 84% non-attrition vs 16% attrition creates modeling challenges - Unknown Variables: Some variables (DailyRate, HourlyRate, MonthlyRate) lack clear definitions - Potential Bias: Data from single organization may not generalize across industries

    Critical Variables Identified

    Through initial analysis, several variables show immediate relevance:

    • OverTime: 28% of employees work overtime, significantly higher than industry norms
    • Job Satisfaction: 40% report low to medium-low satisfaction levels
    • Performance Ratings: Surprisingly, no employees rated below "Excellent" (potential data quality issue)

    Exploratory Data Analysis Strategy

    Analytical Framework

    The EDA follows a structured approach to uncover attrition patterns:

    1. Univariate Analysis: Understanding individual variable distributions
    2. Bivariate Analysis: Relationship between each variable and attrition
    3. Multivariate Analysis: Complex interactions and correlations
    4. Business Context Integration: Translating statistical findings to actionable insights

    Key Findings from Numerical Variables

    Age Distribution: - Near-normal distribution with mean age of 37 years - Good age diversity (18-60 years) indicates inclusive hiring practices - Attrition Insight: Younger employees show higher attrition rates

    Income Analysis: - Mean monthly income: \(6,500 with high variance (\)1,000-$20,000) - Significant income inequality within organization - Critical Finding: Employees leaving earn 30% less than those staying

    Experience Patterns: - Average company tenure: 7 years - Concerning Pattern: Employees leaving have 30% less total work experience - Promotion timing appears problematic (some employees 10+ years without promotion)

    Categorical Variable Insights

    Department Analysis: - R&D: 65% of workforce, 14% attrition rate - Sales: 30% of workforce, 40% attrition rate (major concern) - HR: 5% of workforce, 25% attrition rate

    Work-Life Balance Indicators: - Overtime Workers: 28% of employees, 30%+ attrition rate - Travel Requirements: Frequent travelers show 2x attrition rate - Distance from Home: Employees living farther attrite 16% more

    Statistical Significance

    Strongest Predictors Identified: 1. Overtime status (odds ratio: 2.6x) 2. Monthly income (inverse relationship) 3. Job involvement levels 4. Work-life balance ratings 5. Years since last promotion

    Data Preprocessing & Feature Engineering

    Preprocessing Strategy

    The preprocessing approach balances feature preservation with model requirements:

    Variable Removal Decisions: - EmployeeNumber: Unique identifier with no predictive value - Over18: Single value variable (all employees over 18) - StandardHours: No variation across employees

    Feature Engineering Approach

    Categorical Encoding: - One-Hot Encoding: Applied to multi-category variables (Department, JobRole, etc.) - Binary Mapping: Simple Yes/No variables converted to 1/0 - Ordinal Preservation: Satisfaction and involvement scales maintained as numerical

    Feature Scaling Rationale: - Required for: Logistic Regression and SVM (distance-based algorithms) - Not Required for: Tree-based models (Decision Tree, Random Forest) - Method Used: StandardScaler (Z-score normalization)

    Train-Test Split Strategy

    70-30 Split with Stratification: - Maintains 16% attrition rate in both training and test sets - Ensures model evaluation on representative data - Rationale: Prevents bias from uneven class distribution

    Preprocessing Limitations

    Potential Issues: - Information Loss: One-hot encoding increases dimensionality - Multicollinearity: High correlations between experience-related variables - Feature Selection: No systematic feature selection applied

    Model Development & Comparison

    Algorithm Selection Rationale

    Four algorithms were selected to cover different modeling approaches:

    1. Logistic Regression - Strengths: Interpretable coefficients, probability outputs, baseline model - Use Case: Understanding feature relationships and establishing baseline performance

    2. Support Vector Machine (SVM) - Linear Kernel: Tests linear separability assumption - RBF Kernel: Captures non-linear relationships - Use Case: Handling complex decision boundaries

    3. Decision Tree - Strengths: Highly interpretable, handles non-linear relationships naturally - Weakness: Prone to overfitting - Use Case: Understanding decision pathways

    4. Random Forest - Strengths: Reduces overfitting, feature importance, ensemble robustness - Use Case: Achieving best predictive performance

    Class Imbalance Handling

    Challenge: 84% non-attrition vs 16% attrition creates bias toward majority class

    Solution Applied: - Tree-based models: Class weights {0: 0.17, 1: 0.83} - Linear models: Threshold optimization using precision-recall curves

    Model Performance Summary

    Logistic Regression (Optimized Threshold: 0.35): - Accuracy: 87% - Recall: 74% (significant improvement from 46% at default threshold) - Precision: 50% - Interpretation: Reasonable baseline with clear feature interpretation

    SVM with RBF Kernel (Optimized Threshold: 0.35): - Accuracy: 88% - Recall: 82% (best among all models) - Precision: 52% - Interpretation: Best at identifying true attrition cases

    Decision Tree (Default): - Training Performance: 100% (clear overfitting) - Test Recall: 80% - Test Precision: 73% - Interpretation: Overfits but provides interpretable rules

    Random Forest (Default): - Training Performance: 100% (overfitting) - Test Recall: 80% - Test Precision: 75% - Interpretation: Best overall balance of metrics

    Critical Model Limitations

    Overfitting Concerns: - Tree-based models show perfect training performance - Significant performance drop on test data - Risk: Models may not generalize to new employees

    Precision-Recall Trade-off: - High recall comes at cost of precision - Business Impact: Many false positives means resources spent on employees not at risk

    Feature Interpretation Challenges: - Random Forest feature importance may be unstable - Multicollinearity: Correlated features complicate interpretation

    Performance Evaluation & Optimization

    Evaluation Methodology

    Primary Metric: Recall for Attrition Class - Business Justification: Missing a departing employee is more costly than false alarms - Target: Maximize true positive identification while maintaining reasonable precision

    Threshold Optimization Process

    Precision-Recall Curve Analysis: - Optimal threshold identified at 0.35 for both Logistic Regression and SVM - Trade-off: Higher recall achieved by accepting lower precision - Business Context: Better to over-predict attrition than miss valuable departing employees

    Hyperparameter Tuning Results

    Decision Tree Tuning: - Grid Search Parameters: max_depth, criterion, min_samples_leaf - Result: Tuned model reduces overfitting but sacrifices performance - Precision Drop: From 73% to 29% (concerning for business application)

    Random Forest Tuning: - Grid Search Parameters: n_estimators, min_samples_leaf, max_features - Result: Slight improvement in recall with minimal precision loss - Best Performance: 82% recall, 76% precision on test data

    Feature Importance Analysis

    Consistent Top Predictors Across Models: 1. OverTime: Strongest predictor across all models 2. MonthlyIncome: Inverse relationship with attrition 3. Age: Younger employees higher risk 4. TotalWorkingYears: Experience reduces attrition risk 5. DistanceFromHome: Longer commutes increase risk

    Business Validation: - Feature rankings align with HR domain knowledge - Economic factors (income, overtime) dominate predictions - Work-life balance factors consistently important

    Model Selection Recommendation

    For Production Use: Tuned Random Forest - Rationale: Best balance of recall (82%) and precision (76%) - Reliability: Ensemble method more robust than single models - Interpretability: Feature importance provides actionable insights

    For Business Understanding: Logistic Regression - Rationale: Coefficient interpretation provides odds ratios - Transparency: Clear understanding of factor relationships - Baseline: Establishes performance floor for comparison

    Performance Limitations

    Generalization Concerns: - Single organization data may not transfer to other companies - Industry-specific factors not captured - Temporal stability unknown (model may degrade over time)

    Precision Challenges: - 24% false positive rate means significant resource waste - May cause employee morale issues if targeting becomes obvious - Cost-benefit analysis needed for implementation

    # Calculate business impact metrics
def calculate_business_impact(recall, precision, total_employees=2940):
    """
    Calculate business impact of model deployment.

    Parameters:
    recall: Model recall rate
    precision: Model precision rate
    total_employees: Total employee count

    Returns:
    Dictionary with business impact metrics
    """
    # Assume 16% attrition rate
    actual_attrition = int(total_employees * 0.16)

    # Calculate model predictions
    true_positives = int(actual_attrition * recall)
    false_positives = int((true_positives / precision) - true_positives)
    false_negatives = actual_attrition - true_positives

    # Calculate intervention costs (example values)
    intervention_cost_per_employee = 5000  # Retention incentive cost
    missed_opportunity_cost = 25000  # Cost of losing valued employee

    # Calculate total costs
    intervention_costs = false_positives * intervention_cost_per_employee
    missed_opportunities = false_negatives * missed_opportunity_cost
    successful_retentions = true_positives * intervention_cost_per_employee

    return {
        'employees_correctly_identified': true_positives,
        'employees_incorrectly_targeted': false_positives,
        'employees_missed': false_negatives,
        'intervention_costs': intervention_costs,
        'missed_opportunity_costs': missed_opportunities,
        'retention_investment': successful_retentions,
        'total_cost': intervention_costs + missed_opportunities + successful_retentions
    }

# Example calculation for best model
best_model_impact = calculate_business_impact(recall=0.82, precision=0.76)
print("Business Impact Analysis:")
for key, value in best_model_impact.items():
    print(f"{key}: {value:,}")

    Automated Reporting Function

    def generate_attrition_report(model, X_data, feature_names, threshold=0.5):
    """
    Generate comprehensive attrition risk report for business stakeholders.

    Parameters:
    model: Trained model object
    X_data: Feature data for prediction
    feature_names: List of feature names for interpretation
    threshold: Classification threshold

    Returns:
    DataFrame with employee risk scores and recommendations
    """
    # Generate probability predictions
    if hasattr(model, 'predict_proba'):
        probabilities = model.predict_proba(X_data)[:, 1]
    else:
        # For models without probability output
        probabilities = model.decision_function(X_data)

    # Create risk categories
    def categorize_risk(prob):
        if prob >= 0.7:
            return 'High Risk'
        elif prob >= 0.4:
            return 'Medium Risk'
        else:
            return 'Low Risk'

    # Create report DataFrame
    report_df = pd.DataFrame({
        'employee_id': range(len(X_data)),
        'attrition_probability': probabilities,
        'risk_category': [categorize_risk(p) for p in probabilities],
        'predicted_attrition': probabilities > threshold
    })

    # Sort by risk level
    report_df = report_df.sort_values('attrition_probability', ascending=False)

    # Add recommendations
    def get_recommendation(risk_cat):
        recommendations = {
            'High Risk': 'Immediate manager intervention, retention bonus consideration',
            'Medium Risk': 'Career development discussion, workload review',
            'Low Risk': 'Standard engagement activities'
        }
        return recommendations[risk_cat]

    report_df['recommended_action'] = report_df['risk_category'].apply(get_recommendation)

    return report_df

# Example report generation
sample_report = generate_attrition_report(rf_estimator_tuned, X_test.head(10), X.columns)
print(sample_report)

    Purpose: Create automated reporting system for regular business use and HR decision-making.

    Flow: 1. Generate probability predictions for all employees 2. Categorize risk levels for actionable segmentation 3. Assign specific recommendations based on risk category 4. Create sortable report for HR prioritization

    Impact: Enables regular monitoring and proactive intervention scheduling. Provides clear action items for HR teams.

    Model Monitoring Framework

    def model_performance_monitor(model, X_new, y_new, baseline_metrics):
    """
    Monitor model performance degradation over time.

    Parameters:
    model: Deployed model object
    X_new: New feature data
    y_new: New actual outcomes
    baseline_metrics: Dictionary of baseline performance metrics

    Returns:
    Performance comparison and alerts
    """
    # Generate new predictions
    y_pred_new = model.predict(X_new)

    # Calculate current metrics
    current_accuracy = metrics.accuracy_score(y_new, y_pred_new)
    current_recall = metrics.recall_score(y_new, y_pred_new)
    current_precision = metrics.precision_score(y_new, y_pred_new)

    # Compare with baseline
    accuracy_change = current_accuracy - baseline_metrics['accuracy']
    recall_change = current_recall - baseline_metrics['recall']
    precision_change = current_precision - baseline_metrics['precision']

    # Generate alerts
    alerts = []
    if accuracy_change < -0.05:
        alerts.append("ALERT: Accuracy decreased by more than 5%")
    if recall_change < -0.10:
        alerts.append("ALERT: Recall decreased by more than 10%")
    if precision_change < -0.10:
        alerts.append("ALERT: Precision decreased by more than 10%")

    monitoring_report = {
        'current_accuracy': current_accuracy,
        'current_recall': current_recall,
        'current_precision': current_precision,
        'accuracy_change': accuracy_change,
        'recall_change': recall_change,
        'precision_change': precision_change,
        'alerts': alerts,
        'recommendation': 'Model retraining recommended' if alerts else 'Model performing within acceptable range'
    }

    return monitoring_report

    Flow: 1. Calculate performance metrics on new data 2. Compare with established baseline performance 3. Generate automated alerts for significant degradation 4. Provide retraining recommendations
