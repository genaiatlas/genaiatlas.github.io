    Business Context & Problem Statement

    Industry Challenge

    Coffee roasting represents a critical quality control point in the coffee production chain, where raw green beans undergo thermal transformation to create the final consumable product. The roasting process directly impacts flavor profiles, aroma characteristics, and ultimately market pricing. Traditional quality inspection relies heavily on manual assessment by experienced operators, creating bottlenecks in production scalability and introducing subjective variability in quality standards.

    KC Roasters' Automation Initiative

    KC Roasters, a commercial coffee roasting operation, faces the challenge of scaling quality inspection while maintaining consistency. Their roasting infrastructure employs a five-chamber system with sophisticated temperature monitoring - three sensors per chamber providing granular thermal data throughout the roasting process. The company seeks to implement predictive analytics to automate quality assessment, reducing human intervention while achieving or exceeding human-level accuracy.

    Technical Problem Definition

    Objective: Develop a regression model to predict coffee bean quality scores (0-100 scale) based on multi-sensor temperature data, material volume, and humidity measurements.

    Business Impact: Automated quality prediction enables:

    • Real-time production adjustments

    • Consistent pricing strategies based on predicted quality

    • Reduced dependency on subjective human assessment

    • Scalable quality control for increased production volumes

    • Predictive maintenance insights for roasting equipment

    Data Architecture: 17 predictor variables capturing thermal profiles across five roasting chambers, plus material characteristics, targeting continuous quality scores.

    Data Overview & Structure

    Dataset Characteristics

    The roasting quality dataset contains 29,131 observations across 18 variables representing a comprehensive view of the roasting process. The data structure reflects the industrial IoT sensor deployment across KC Roasters' five-chamber system.

    Variable Architecture

    Temperature Sensors (15 variables):

    • T_data_X_Y: Temperature readings in Fahrenheit where X = chamber number (1-5), Y = sensor position (1-3)

    • Chamber progression represents the roasting timeline, with temperatures generally increasing through chambers

    • Multiple sensors per chamber capture spatial temperature variation within large industrial chambers

    Material Characteristics (2 variables):

    • H_data: Raw material height/volume entering chambers (pounds) - affects heat distribution and roasting uniformity

    • AH_data: Relative humidity of roasted beans - critical for final product stability and flavor preservation

    Target Variable:

    • quality: Continuous score (0-100) representing overall bean quality assessment

    Data Quality Assessment

    Missing Values: Limited missing data in H_data and AH_data (material characteristics), suggesting sensor reliability issues rather than systematic collection problems.

    Data Types: Primarily numerical data (16 integer, 2 float variables) - appropriate for regression modeling without extensive categorical encoding requirements.

    Scale Considerations: Temperature variables span different ranges across chambers, indicating the thermal progression through the roasting process and necessitating careful feature scaling strategies.

    Industrial Context Insights

    The multi-sensor approach reflects real-world manufacturing complexity where:

    • Spatial Variation: Large industrial chambers experience uneven heat distribution

    • Process Monitoring: Sequential chamber design allows progressive roasting control

    • Quality Correlation: Temperature profiles likely exhibit non-linear relationships with quality outcomes

    Exploratory Data Analysis

    Temperature Distribution Analysis

    Chamber 1 Anomaly Detection: Initial analysis revealed significant outliers in the first temperature sensor of Chamber 1, with minimum readings as low as 13°F while other sensors in the same chamber recorded minimum temperatures of 168-183°F. This pattern suggests sensor calibration issues or equipment malfunction rather than legitimate process variation.

    Resolution Strategy: Implemented data clipping to align outlier values with the minimum credible temperature threshold (168°F) based on physical constraints of the roasting process.

    Chamber Progression Patterns:

    • Chamber 1: Entry point temperatures (150-300°F) - initial heating phase

    • Chamber 2: Peak thermal activity (250-400°F) - primary roasting occurs

    • Chamber 3: High-temperature maintenance (400-600°F) - flavor development

    • Chambers 4-5: Controlled cooling (200-450°F) - process completion and stabilization

    Quality Distribution Characteristics

    Target Variable Analysis: The quality scores exhibit a left-skewed distribution with most products achieving scores between 20-100, indicating that the roasting process generally produces acceptable quality outcomes with few catastrophic failures.

    Business Implication: The skewed distribution suggests that the primary modeling challenge is distinguishing between "good" and "excellent" quality rather than identifying failures, which aligns with KC Roasters' goal of premium product optimization.

    Bivariate Relationship Insights

    Temperature-Quality Correlations:

    • Chamber 2 Significance: Temperature ranges of 300-400°F in Chamber 2 show strongest positive correlation with quality scores, identifying this as the critical roasting phase

    • Chamber 4 Impact: Temperatures in the 200-450°F range correlate with higher quality, confirming the importance of controlled cooling

    • Limited Impact Chambers: Chambers 1, 3, and 5 show weaker direct relationships with quality, though they likely contribute to overall thermal profile

    Material Characteristics:

    • Volume Independence: H_data (material height) shows minimal correlation with quality within the typical operating range (150-200 pounds), suggesting robust process control

    • Humidity Stability: AH_data (relative humidity) remains relatively stable (5-10%) across quality levels, indicating consistent post-roasting handling

    Critical Limitations Identified

    Sensor Reliability Concerns: The presence of significant outliers in temperature sensors raises questions about measurement accuracy and the need for robust preprocessing strategies.

    Correlation vs. Causation: While temperature correlations are identified, the analysis doesn't establish causal relationships, limiting the model's ability to guide process optimization.

    Temporal Dynamics Missing: The current dataset lacks time-series information, preventing analysis of roasting duration effects on quality.

    Data Preprocessing Strategy

    Outlier Management Approach

    Temperature Sensor Calibration: Based on EDA findings, implemented targeted outlier correction for Chambers 1 and 2 where first sensors showed implausible readings below the minimum threshold established by co-located sensors.

    Rationale: Rather than removing outliers (which could eliminate valuable process variation), the approach clips extreme values to physically plausible ranges while preserving the underlying data structure.

    Limitations: This approach assumes that co-located sensors provide reliable baselines, which may not hold if multiple sensors experience simultaneous drift.

    Missing Value Imputation Strategy

    Method Selection: Median imputation for H_data and AH_data variables based on:

    • Robustness: Median provides stability against remaining outliers

    • Process Logic: Material characteristics likely follow consistent operational patterns

    • Data Distribution: Non-normal distributions make mean imputation inappropriate

    Implementation Timing: Imputation performed after train-test split to prevent data leakage, ensuring that validation and test sets don't influence the imputation parameters derived from training data.

    Alternative Considerations: More sophisticated imputation methods (KNN, iterative) were not explored, potentially missing opportunities to leverage relationships between variables for more accurate imputation.

    Data Splitting Strategy

    Three-Way Split Approach:

    • Training Set: 60% for model development

    • Validation Set: 20% for hyperparameter tuning and model selection

    • Test Set: 20% for final performance evaluation

    Rationale: The large dataset size (29,131 observations) supports this three-way split without compromising statistical power, while providing robust evaluation capabilities.

    Stratification Considerations: No stratification was implemented for the continuous target variable, which could lead to slight distributional differences between splits, particularly given the left-skewed quality distribution.

    Feature Engineering Limitations

    Missed Opportunities:

    • Temperature Gradients: No calculation of temperature differences between chambers or sensors

    • Thermal Stability Metrics: No analysis of temperature variance within chambers

    • Process Efficiency Indicators: No derived features combining material volume with thermal profiles

    • Domain Knowledge Integration: Limited incorporation of coffee roasting science principles

    Scaling Requirements: While not implemented in the preprocessing phase, the varying temperature ranges across chambers suggest that feature scaling could improve model performance, particularly for distance-based algorithms.

    Preprocessing Pipeline Validation

    Strength: The use of scikit-learn pipelines in the final implementation ensures reproducible preprocessing and prevents data leakage during production deployment.

    Concern: The preprocessing steps were developed based on training data insights but may not generalize well to new operational conditions or equipment configurations.

    Model Development & Comparison

    Algorithm Selection Rationale

    The modeling approach employed six regression algorithms, each selected to capture different aspects of the temperature-quality relationship:

    Tree-Based Methods:

    • Decision Tree: Baseline model for capturing non-linear temperature thresholds

    • Random Forest: Ensemble approach for improved generalization and feature importance insights

    • Bagging Regressor: Variance reduction through bootstrap aggregation

    Boosting Methods:

    • AdaBoost: Sequential error correction for complex pattern learning

    • Gradient Boosting: Advanced sequential optimization for non-linear relationships

    • XGBoost: Optimized gradient boosting with regularization

    Initial Performance Analysis

    Decision Tree Results: - Training Performance: R² = 1.000, RMSE = 0.051 - Validation Performance: R² = 0.851, RMSE = 4.59

    Critical Assessment: Severe overfitting with perfect training performance but substantial validation degradation. The model memorized training patterns without learning generalizable relationships.

    Random Forest Results: - Training Performance: R² = 0.985, RMSE = 1.46
    - Validation Performance: R² = 0.912, RMSE = 3.54

    Performance Interpretation: Strong performance with manageable overfitting. The ensemble approach effectively reduced variance while maintaining predictive power.

    Bagging Regressor Results: - Training Performance: R² = 0.978, RMSE = 1.76 - Validation Performance: R² = 0.911, RMSE = 3.57

    Analysis: Comparable to Random Forest with slightly better generalization characteristics, suggesting that bootstrap aggregation effectively captures the underlying temperature-quality relationships.

    Boosting Algorithm Challenges

    AdaBoost Limitations: - Training Performance: R² = 0.754, RMSE = 5.91 - Validation Performance: R² = 0.745, RMSE = 6.03

    Interpretation: Poor performance suggests that sequential error correction may not be suitable for this problem structure, possibly due to the complexity of temperature interactions.

    Gradient Boosting & XGBoost: Both algorithms showed suboptimal performance (R² ≈ 0.85, RMSE ≈ 4.6-4.8), indicating that default hyperparameters were insufficient for this specific problem domain.

    Model Selection Insights

    Top Performers Identified:

    1. Random Forest: Best balance of performance and interpretability

    2. Bagging Regressor: Strong generalization with computational efficiency

    3. Decision Tree: High variance but potential for improvement through tuning

    Eliminated Candidates: Boosting algorithms were excluded from hyperparameter tuning due to poor initial performance, though this decision may have prematurely eliminated potentially viable approaches.

    Performance Evaluation Limitations

    Metric Selection: Primary reliance on R² and RMSE may not capture business-relevant performance characteristics. Quality prediction errors at different score ranges may have varying business impacts.

    Cross-Validation Absence: Single train-validation split for initial comparison may not provide robust performance estimates, particularly given potential temporal or operational dependencies in the data.

    Feature Importance Neglect: Initial model comparison focused on predictive accuracy without considering feature importance consistency across algorithms, missing opportunities for ensemble insights.

    Hyperparameter Optimization

    Optimization Strategy

    RandomizedSearchCV Implementation: The hyperparameter tuning employed randomized search rather than exhaustive grid search, balancing computational efficiency with thorough parameter exploration. This approach is particularly valuable given the large dataset size and multiple algorithms under consideration.

    Cross-Validation Framework: 5-fold cross-validation provided robust performance estimation during hyperparameter optimization, reducing the risk of overfitting to specific validation set characteristics.

    Random Forest Optimization

    Parameter Space Explored:

    • max_depth: [4, 6, 8, 10, None] - controlling tree complexity and overfitting

    • max_features: ['sqrt', 'log2', None] - managing feature selection randomness

    • n_estimators: [80, 90, 100, 110, 120] - balancing performance and computational cost

    Optimal Configuration: - max_depth: None (unlimited depth) - max_features: 'log2' (logarithmic feature selection)
    - n_estimators: 110 (moderate ensemble size)

    Performance Improvement: - Validation R²: Improved from 0.912 to 0.925 - Validation RMSE: Reduced from 3.54 to 3.26

    Analysis: The unlimited max_depth parameter suggests that the underlying temperature-quality relationships are sufficiently complex to benefit from deep trees, while log2 feature selection provides optimal randomness for generalization.

    Bagging Regressor Optimization

    Parameter Space:

    • max_samples: [0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1] - controlling bootstrap sample size

    • max_features: [0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1] - managing feature randomness

    • n_estimators: [50, 100, 120, 150] - ensemble size optimization

    Optimal Configuration: - max_samples: 0.7 (70% bootstrap sampling) - max_features: 0.9 (90% feature utilization) - n_estimators: 120 (large ensemble)

    Performance Impact: - Validation R²: Improved from 0.911 to 0.918 - Validation RMSE: Reduced from 3.57 to 3.42

    Interpretation: The 70% bootstrap sampling suggests that aggressive subsampling improves generalization, while high feature utilization (90%) indicates that most temperature sensors contribute valuable information.

    Decision Tree Optimization Challenges

    Extensive Parameter Search:

    • max_depth: [15, 16, 17, 18, 19, None]

    • min_samples_leaf: [1, 3, None]

    • max_leaf_nodes: [5, 10, 15, None]

    • min_impurity_decrease: [0.001, 0.0]

    Optimal Configuration: - max_depth: None - min_samples_leaf: 1 - max_leaf_nodes: None
    - min_impurity_decrease: 0.001

    Persistent Overfitting: Despite extensive tuning, the Decision Tree continued to exhibit severe overfitting with perfect training performance but poor validation generalization (R² = 0.842, RMSE = 4.74).

    Structural Limitation: Single decision trees appear fundamentally unsuitable for this problem, likely due to the complex, non-linear interactions between multiple temperature sensors that require ensemble methods for effective modeling.

    Optimization Strategy Limitations

    Computational Constraints: The randomized search approach, while efficient, may have missed optimal parameter combinations that exhaustive search could have identified.

    Limited Parameter Ranges: The parameter spaces explored were relatively conservative and may not have captured the full potential of each algorithm.

    Metric Optimization: Optimization focused solely on R² score without considering business-specific loss functions that might weight different types of prediction errors differently.

    Algorithm Abandonment: The decision to exclude boosting algorithms from hyperparameter tuning may have been premature, as these algorithms often require more extensive tuning to achieve optimal performance.

    Final Model Selection & Validation

    Model Performance Comparison

    Final Algorithm Rankings:

    1. Tuned Random Forest: R² = 0.925, RMSE = 3.26, MAE = 2.45

    2. Tuned Bagging Regressor: R² = 0.918, RMSE = 3.42, MAE = 2.58

    3. Tuned Decision Tree: R² = 0.842, RMSE = 4.74, MAE = 3.56

    Selection Rationale: Random Forest emerged as the optimal model based on:

    • Superior Predictive Accuracy: Highest R² and lowest RMSE across all evaluation metrics

    • Robust Generalization: Minimal overfitting with consistent train-validation performance

    • Feature Importance Insights: Built-in interpretability for understanding temperature sensor contributions

    • Production Readiness: Mature algorithm with reliable scikit-learn implementation

    Test Set Validation

    Unseen Data Performance: - Test R²: 0.901 - Test RMSE: 3.74
    - Test MAE: 2.81

    Performance Interpretation: The test set results confirm model generalization capability, with performance metrics falling between training and validation results. The slight performance degradation from validation (R² 0.925 to 0.901) is within acceptable bounds and suggests robust model development.

    Business Context Translation: - Mean Absolute Error of 2.81: On average, quality predictions deviate by 2.81 points on the 0-100 scale

    • R² of 0.901: The model explains 90.1% of quality variance, leaving 9.9% unexplained

    • Practical Accuracy: For a product with true quality of 80, predicted range would typically be 77-83

    Feature Importance Analysis

    Critical Temperature Sensors:

    1. T_data_3_2: Most important predictor (Chamber 3, Sensor 2)

    2. T_data_3_1: Second most important (Chamber 3, Sensor 1)

    3. T_data_4_2: Third most important (Chamber 4, Sensor 2)

    Process Insights: The dominance of Chamber 3 and 4 sensors aligns with coffee roasting science, where:

    • Chamber 3: Primary flavor development phase requiring precise temperature control

    • Chamber 4: Critical cooling phase affecting final product stability

    • Secondary Sensors: Sensors 1 and 2 capture spatial temperature variation within chambers

    Feature Engineering Implications: The importance pattern suggests that temperature gradients and chamber-to-chamber differences might provide additional predictive value through feature engineering.

    Model Limitations and Risks

    Performance Constraints:

    • Quality Range Limitation: Model performs well within observed quality range (20-100) but may extrapolate poorly beyond this range

    • Sensor Dependency: Model requires all 15 temperature sensors operational; sensor failures could degrade performance

    • Temporal Assumptions: Model assumes stable relationships over time; equipment aging or process changes could impact accuracy

    Business Risk Assessment:

    • Conservative Estimates: MAE of 2.81 may be acceptable for pricing decisions but could impact premium product classification

    • Edge Case Performance: Model may struggle with unusual operating conditions not represented in training data

    • Feature Drift: Changes in equipment calibration or operational procedures could gradually degrade model performance

    Recommended Monitoring: Continuous performance monitoring with automatic model retraining when prediction accuracy degrades below acceptable business thresholds.

    def histogram_boxplot(data, feature, figsize=(12, 7), kde=False, bins=None):
    """
    Boxplot and histogram combined for comprehensive univariate analysis

    Parameters:
    -----------
    data : DataFrame
        Input dataset containing the feature
    feature : str
        Column name to analyze
    figsize : tuple, default (12,7)
        Figure size for the plot
    kde : bool, default False
        Whether to show kernel density estimate
    bins : int, optional
        Number of histogram bins

    Returns:
    --------
    matplotlib figure with boxplot and histogram subplots
    """
    f2, (ax_box2, ax_hist2) = plt.subplots(
        nrows=2,  # Number of rows of the subplot grid = 2
        sharex=True,  # X-axis will be shared among all subplots
        gridspec_kw={"height_ratios": (0.25, 0.75)},
        figsize=figsize,
    )  # creating the 2 subplots

    sns.boxplot(
        data=data, x=feature, ax=ax_box2, showmeans=True, color="violet"
    )  # boxplot will be created and a triangle will indicate the mean value

    sns.histplot(
        data=data, x=feature, kde=kde, ax=ax_hist2, bins=bins, palette="winter"
    ) if bins else sns.histplot(
        data=data, x=feature, kde=kde, ax=ax_hist2

    )  # Add mean to the histogram
    ax_hist2.axvline(
        data[feature].median(), color="black", linestyle="-"
    )  # Add median to the histogram

    Purpose: Creates standardized visualization combining boxplot and histogram for comprehensive univariate analysis of each variable.

    Flow: - Subplot Creation: 2-row layout with shared x-axis for consistent scaling - Boxplot: Shows quartiles, outliers, and mean (triangle marker) - Histogram: Shows distribution shape with optional KDE overlay - Reference Lines: Vertical lines for mean (green dashed) and median (black solid)

    def adj_r2_score(predictors, targets, predictions):
    """
    Calculate adjusted R-squared score for regression models

    Parameters:
    -----------
    predictors : array-like
        Feature matrix used for predictions
    targets : array-like
        True target values
    predictions : array-like
        Predicted target values

    Returns:
    --------
    float : Adjusted R-squared score

    Mathematical Foundation:
    Adjusted R² = 1 - [(1-R²)(n-1)/(n-k-1)]
    where n = number of observations, k = number of predictors
    """
    r2 = r2_score(targets, predictions)
    n = predictors.shape[0]  # number of observations
    k = predictors.shape[1]  # number of predictors
    return 1 - ((1 - r2) * (n - 1) / (n - k - 1))

    Purpose: Calculates adjusted R-squared that penalizes model complexity, providing more accurate model comparison than standard R-squared.

    Flow: - Standard R²: Measures proportion of variance explained - Penalty Term: Adjusts for number of predictors and observations - Complexity Punishment: Higher penalty for more features relative to sample size

    Impact: Prevents selection of overly complex models with marginal predictive improvement. Essential for comparing models with different complexity levels.

    def model_performance_regression(model, predictors, target):
    """
    Comprehensive regression model performance evaluation

    Parameters:
    -----------
    model : sklearn estimator
        Trained regression model
    predictors : DataFrame/array
        Feature matrix for prediction
    target : Series/array
        True target values

    Returns:
    --------
    DataFrame : Performance metrics including RMSE, MAE, R², Adjusted R²
    """
    # Generate predictions
    pred = model.predict(predictors)

    # Calculate performance metrics
    r2 = r2_score(target, pred)  # Coefficient of determination
    adjr2 = adj_r2_score(predictors, target, pred)  # Adjusted R-squared
    rmse = np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(target, pred))  # Root Mean Square Error
    mae = mean_absolute_error(target, pred)  # Mean Absolute Error

    # Create results DataFrame
    df_perf = pd.DataFrame({
        "RMSE": rmse,
        "MAE": mae,
        "R-squared": r2,
        "Adj. R-squared": adjr2,
    }, index=[0])

    return df_perf

    Purpose: Standardizes performance evaluation across all models with comprehensive metrics suite.

    Flow: - Prediction Generation: Uses trained model to predict on provided data - Metrics Calculation: Computes four key regression metrics - Result Formatting: Returns organized DataFrame for easy comparison

    Impact: Ensures consistent evaluation methodology across all algorithms. Multiple metrics provide different perspectives on model performance.

    class CoffeeQualityPredictor:
    """
    Production-ready coffee quality prediction system

    Attributes:
    -----------
    model : sklearn.pipeline.Pipeline
        Trained prediction pipeline including preprocessing
    feature_names : list
        Expected feature names for input validation
    performance_baseline : dict
        Baseline performance metrics for monitoring
    """

    def __init__(self, model_pipeline, feature_names, baseline_performance):
        self.model = model_pipeline
        self.feature_names = feature_names
        self.baseline = baseline_performance
        self.prediction_log = []

    def validate_input(self, input_data):
        """
        Validate input data structure and completeness

        Parameters:
        -----------
        input_data : DataFrame
            Sensor readings and process parameters

        Returns:
        --------
        bool : True if valid, raises exception if invalid
        """
        # Check column completeness
        if not all(col in input_data.columns for col in self.feature_names):
            missing_cols = set(self.feature_names) - set(input_data.columns)
            raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {missing_cols}")

        # Check data types
        numeric_cols = input_data.select_dtypes(include=[np.number]).columns
        if len(numeric_cols) != len(self.feature_names):
            raise ValueError("All features must be numeric")

        # Check for reasonable value ranges
        temp_cols = [col for col in input_data.columns if col.startswith('T_data')]
        if (input_data[temp_cols] < 0).any().any():
            raise ValueError("Temperature values cannot be negative")

        return True

    def predict_quality(self, sensor_data):
        """
        Generate quality prediction with confidence assessment

        Parameters:
        -----------
        sensor_data : DataFrame
            Real-time sensor readings

        Returns:
        --------
        dict : Prediction results with confidence metrics
        """
        # Validate input
        self.validate_input(sensor_data)

        # Generate prediction
        quality_prediction = self.model.predict(sensor_data)

        # Calculate prediction confidence (simplified example)
        # In production, this would use more sophisticated uncertainty quantification
        feature_importance = self.model.named_steps['Random forest'].feature_importances_
        data_completeness = 1 - sensor_data.isnull().sum().sum() / (len(sensor_data.columns) * len(sensor_data))
        confidence_score = min(data_completeness * 100, 95)

        # Log prediction for monitoring
        prediction_record = {
            'timestamp': pd.Timestamp.now(),
            'prediction': quality_prediction[0],
            'confidence': confidence_score,
            'input_data_hash': hash(str(sensor_data.values.tobytes()))
        }
        self.prediction_log.append(prediction_record)

        return {
            'quality_score': round(quality_prediction[0], 2),
            'confidence_percentage': round(confidence_score, 1),
            'quality_tier': self.classify_quality_tier(quality_prediction[0]),
            'timestamp': prediction_record['timestamp']
        }

    def classify_quality_tier(self, quality_score):
        """
        Convert quality score to business tier classification

        Parameters:
        -----------
        quality_score : float
            Predicted quality score (0-100)

        Returns:
        --------
        str : Quality tier classification
        """
        if quality_score >= 90:
            return "Premium"
        elif quality_score >= 70:
            return "Standard"
        elif quality_score >= 50:
            return "Budget"
        else:
            return "Substandard"

    def monitor_performance(self, actual_quality_scores, prediction_window=100):
        """
        Monitor prediction accuracy against actual quality assessments

        Parameters:
        -----------
        actual_quality_scores : list
            True quality scores for recent predictions
        prediction_window : int
            Number of recent predictions to analyze

        Returns:
        --------
        dict : Performance monitoring results
        """
        if len(self.prediction_log) < prediction_window:
            return {"status": "Insufficient data for monitoring"}

        recent_predictions = [log['prediction'] for log in self.prediction_log[-prediction_window:]]

        if len(actual_quality_scores) != len(recent_predictions):
            return {"status": "Mismatch between predictions and actual scores"}

        # Calculate current performance metrics
        current_mae = mean_absolute_error(actual_quality_scores, recent_predictions)
        current_r2 = r2_score(actual_quality_scores, recent_predictions)

        # Compare to baseline
        performance_status = {
            "current_mae": round(current_mae, 3),
            "baseline_mae": self.baseline.get("mae", 0),
            "mae_degradation": current_mae > (self.baseline.get("mae", 0) * 1.2),
            "current_r2": round(current_r2, 3),
            "baseline_r2": self.baseline.get("r2", 0),
            "r2_degradation": current_r2 < (self.baseline.get("r2", 0) * 0.9),
            "recommendation": "Monitor" if current_mae <= (self.baseline.get("mae", 0) * 1.2) else "Retrain"
        }

        return performance_status

    Purpose: Provides production-ready class for deploying quality prediction system with built-in monitoring and validation.

    Flow: - Input Validation: Ensures data quality and completeness - Prediction Generation: Applies trained pipeline with confidence assessment - Quality Classification: Converts scores to business-relevant tiers - Performance Monitoring: Tracks accuracy against baseline metrics

    Impact: Enables reliable production deployment with automatic quality assurance and performance tracking.
